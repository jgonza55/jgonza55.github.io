Research

My research lies in the field of computer algebra, with two main focuses: regular chains and multivariate formal Puiseux series.

Regular chains

Regular chains are triangular sets of polynomials used to solve systems of polynomial equations. My PhD thesis, Contributions to the Decomposition of Polynomial Systems in Positive Dimension, studies regular chains of dimension one, that is, regular chains where the number of equations is one less than the number of variables. In particular, I developed modular algorithms that compute the intersection between a one-dimensional structure and a hypersurface by means of evaluation and interpolation.

Multivariate Puiseux series

Multivariate Puiseux series are a generalization of multivariate power series in which we allow rational exponents in our variables. I implemented multivariate formal Puiseux series in Maple using lazy evaluation, where coefficients are generated on demand. This supports Puiseux series expansion and the factorization of univariate polynomials with Puiseux series coefficients, based on the Puiseux theorem.

Projects

  • Core developer for five years of the Basic Polynomial Algebra Subprograms (BPAS), a high-performance C/C++ library for solving systems of non-linear polynomial equations. I implemented a modular evaluation-interpolation algorithm for triangular decompositions that runs up to 125× faster than the non-modular solver on benchmark systems, and multivariate rational function reconstruction that is up to 7.7× faster than the classical Ducos algorithm on resultant benchmarks.
  • One of the developers of Maple’s MultivariatePowerSeries package, where I implemented the core algorithms for multivariate Puiseux series and wrote the documentation for all Puiseux series commands.
